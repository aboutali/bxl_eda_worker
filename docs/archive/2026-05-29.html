Today

The EU Council adopted two parallel Middle East sanctions packages, listing four entities and three individuals under its West Bank settler-violence regime and adding ten names plus a broadened framework against Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad — moves Bern will need to weigh for autonomous alignment, alongside the extension of the EU's Russia human-rights sanctions regime to May 2027. The settler listings, unveiled around the Gymnich informal FAC where HR/VP Kallas briefed press, were dismissed as symbolic by Israel-critical capitals but coincide with Guterres placing Israel on the UN conflict-related sexual violence blacklist, sharpening Israel's isolation. On the eastern flank, a Russian drone from Ukraine struck a residential block in Galați, Romania, drawing condemnation from EU leaders and NATO SG Rutte, while Zelensky travelled to Stockholm to negotiate a defence package potentially including Gripen jets. Brussels also reopened two contentious internal files: von der Leyen pressed for tougher China trade defences against member-state resistance as commissioners held a strategic debate on Beijing, and meets Orbán Friday over €10.4bn in frozen Hungarian funds tied to Russia and Ukraine disputes. INTA's June agenda includes the vote on legislation implementing the August 2025 EU-US 'Turnberry' tariff commitments — a file with direct read-across for Swiss market access calculations.
