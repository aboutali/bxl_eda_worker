Today

Iran and Israel resumed direct strikes, collapsing the two-month April ceasefire after Tehran fired missiles at Israeli territory and Israel responded with airstrikes on Iranian military targets; Netanyahu pressed ahead despite Trump's demand for an immediate halt, with Washington floating both a two-to-three-day window for a nuclear and Hormuz deal and, alternately, ground troops. The reopened front is already feeding into European energy-price anxieties, flagged in Berlin alongside FCAS deliberations, and analysts read Tehran's move as a doctrinal shift toward preemptive retaliation following the Israeli strike on Beirut. On the Russia track, HR/VP Kallas travelled to Dublin to confront Ireland over a Russian-owned alumina refinery still exporting to Russia — a sanctions-circumvention file Bern will want to track given Swiss alignment with EU restrictive measures — while the EU disbursed close to €2.8 billion to Kyiv and delivered a UNSC statement reiterating support for Ukraine, a text Switzerland could plausibly co-sign. Separately, Berlin and Paris floated an "associate membership" formula to unblock enlargement frustrations over Ukraine and the Western Balkans, and Commissioner Kos travelled to Kyiv to back accession-linked reforms.
