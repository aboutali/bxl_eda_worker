Today

Trump and Iranian President Pezeshkian signed a 14-point US-Iran framework agreement overnight at Versailles, reportedly ending hostilities, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, releasing frozen Iranian funds in exchange for conditional sanctions relief, and committing to roughly $300bn in reconstruction while accepting Tehran retains ballistic missiles; follow-up negotiations are set for Friday at the Bürgenstock, where Bern (Parmelin confirming preparations) retains a mediating role despite the ceremonial signature being pulled forward, with direct implications for Swiss sanctions architecture on Iran and the handling of blocked assets. In parallel, the EU adopted a new Russia sanctions package targeting energy revenues, the military-industrial base, propaganda outlets and Ukraine-war human rights violations — a measure Switzerland will need to assess for autonomous alignment — while G7 foreign ministers at Lake Geneva and leaders in Évian closed ranks on Ukraine and Iran, with the EU pushing back on a US critical-minerals pricing proposal and securing additional Ukraine sanctions and defence commitments. Brussels also opened the first cluster of EU accession negotiations with Kyiv, though the FT flags divisions among capitals over the pace, as leaders convene in Brussels to debate tougher trade measures against China amid a projected €360bn 2025 deficit. On the battlefield, Ukraine struck Moscow with nearly 200 drones, hitting the capital's largest refinery.
