Today

The EU adopted its 21st Russia sanctions package, targeting energy, financial services and crypto to further constrain war financing, though member-state carve-outs—a Greek LNG/gas-transport exemption, Austrian concessions and continued Russian cod imports—diluted the measures and exposed divisions in Russia policy; Bern will need to weigh the scope of its own alignment given those gaps, as the package coincided with intensified Russian strikes on Odessa ports and a Ukrainian drone attack near Saint Petersburg. In the Gulf, US strikes on Iranian coastal and military infrastructure near the Strait of Hormuz (Ahvaz, Bandar Abbas, Qeshm) reached a reported thirteenth round amid Trump deliberations over a larger operation, while Houthi attacks on two Saudi tankers closed a second Red Sea chokepoint, pushing Brent above $100 and triggering equity and bond selloffs. The ECB held rates steady while monitoring the energy shock and war-driven inflation. HR/VP Kallas condemned the Houthi threats to Saudi Arabia and freedom of navigation—a statement Switzerland may consider co-signing—alongside her engagements at the EU-ASEAN ministerial. Separately, the Commission fined Google €890 million over search and app-store antitrust practices, ordering remedial changes.
