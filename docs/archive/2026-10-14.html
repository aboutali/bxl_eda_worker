Today

The EU adopted its largest sanctions package to date, listing close to 1,600 individuals and entities tied to Russia's military-industrial complex, alongside a separate Council listing of 77 people for organizing Russian State Duma elections in occupied Ukrainian territories—both present alignment decisions for Bern. The measures coincided with a Foreign Affairs Council session and a post-meeting press conference by HR/VP Kallas, and ran against trans-Atlantic friction after Trump signaled plans to permit Russian diesel purchases, a move UK officials flagged as straining allied unity and which, with cold weather approaching, pushed European gas prices to levels last seen during Iran-related tensions. Brussels reinforced its Ukraine commitment by disbursing €2.86 billion under the defence tranche of the €90 billion Support Loan and opening accession-negotiation clusters with Ukraine and Moldova, with further accession conferences including Albania set for 13 October. Hybrid-warfare signals multiplied: alleged Russian Starlink sabotage during a major attack on Ukraine, drone strikes on two ships in Bulgarian Black Sea waters raising NATO response-threshold questions, and Slovakia's restoration of military-diplomatic ties with Moscow drawing espionage warnings. In the Middle East, Houthi forces struck Saudi Aramco headquarters in retaliation for coalition strikes on Sanaa, while Iranian hardliners escalated pressure on Pezeshkian's government, demanding Foreign Minister Araghchi's removal amid deepening economic and wartime strain.
