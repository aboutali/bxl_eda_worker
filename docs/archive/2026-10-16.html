Today

EU leaders reconvened in Brussels for a European Council summit dominated by Ukraine financing, with Zelensky in attendance pressing for additional defence funding and further sanctions as warnings mount that Kyiv is running short of money; the Commission separately disbursed €2.86 billion for drones, missiles and battlefield needs, while leaders made little headway on the multiannual financial framework, strategic gas reserves and the Emissions Trading System. The summit coincided with a widening transatlantic gap: Washington is weighing relief on Russian and Iranian shadow-fleet tankers to curb fuel prices before the midterms and proposed unfreezing some immobilised Russian assets to restart peace talks—met with European resistance at a Miami meeting—alongside a US move to import Russian diesel that strains alignment on Moscow. On the restrictive-measures side, the Council listed three individuals and nine entities for sanctions circumvention across imports, energy and finance, a package Bern will need to assess for alignment, as the EEAS condemned Russian strikes on Ukraine's trade routes and grain exports. The summit is also expected to address attacks on shipping off Bulgaria, with Estonia relocating troops to its Russian border, while accession negotiations opened with Ukraine and Moldova on internal-market and competitiveness clusters. For Swiss tracking, the Sudanese gold-import ban remains relevant as output rises despite the civil war, and analysts flag that a Ukraine ceasefire could redirect Russian pressure toward Europe, including Switzerland.
