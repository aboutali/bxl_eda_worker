Today

Washington has announced tariffs of up to 15 percent on Switzerland, the EU and China, part of a wider round that also targets Japan and South Korea and puts Bern and Brussels under parallel trade pressure; the Federal Council is withholding a response until it receives official notification. On Russia, von der Leyen announced a 22nd sanctions package, and the Council added 15 entities supporting Russia's military-industrial complex to the Annex IV export-restriction list, both measures Switzerland will need to assess for alignment. The Commission also issued implementing guidelines for a Schengen visa ban on individuals who fought in Russia's war, which bears directly on Switzerland as a Schengen associated state. Separately, Pyongyang closed Switzerland's newly opened diplomatic office and gave its head, Sylvain Astier, 48 hours to leave, calling Swiss sanctions on the Songdowon International Children's Camp a "blatant hostile act." In Brussels, von der Leyen told MEPs an energy ceasefire would test Putin's seriousness on peace and that a European team will join the next round of Ukraine talks, while a special summit on the next MFF exposed splits over its size, new own resources and Ukraine funding, with Merz demanding that all 27 member states contribute to Kyiv.
