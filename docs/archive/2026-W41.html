This week

The week's dominant thread was US pressure on Kyiv: Trump urged Ukraine to replace Zelenskyy, threatened to cut intelligence-sharing over strikes on Russian refineries, and announced a diesel deal with Putin that eases Russia sanctions to lower domestic fuel prices ahead of the US midterms—moves Zelenskyy linked to renewed Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia and Kyiv, and which complicate the Western sanctions architecture Bern tracks for alignment. In a parallel transatlantic rupture, Washington imposed sanctions on the International Criminal Court, prompting a statement from the EU High Representative and a Commission reaffirmation of support for the Court; Switzerland should weigh co-signing, given the concurrent Nobel Peace Prize to Geneva-based jurist Navi Pillay, who chaired the UN inquiry accusing Israel of genocide in Gaza. The EU Council separately prolonged restrictive measures against Russian hybrid and destabilising activities until October 2027—a file for Swiss alignment—as European spy agencies warned of intensifying Russian sabotage and assassination plots against defence-industry executives, and the Commission disbursed roughly €1.24 billion to Ukraine for drones and missiles. In the Middle East, US Central Command struck a tanker in the Gulf of Oman enforcing its blockade of Iranian ports, drawing an EU aviation warning, while a Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's King Khalid airport killed at least twelve. Looking ahead, Commissioner Šefčovič held second trade consultations in Beijing amid tariff tensions and a Macron-Merz push on Chinese imports, the Foreign Affairs Council meets 12 October, and the European Council convenes 15–16 October on Ukraine, the Middle East, defence, the MFF, migration, and enlargement.
