This week

The EU adopted its largest sanctions listings to date, targeting close to 1,600 individuals and entities linked to Russia's military-industrial complex, alongside additional designations for circumvention of existing measures — both presenting near-term alignment questions for Bern. The package coincided with the 15-16 October European Council, which reaffirmed Ukraine support and issued conclusions on the Middle East, defence, enlargement, migration and the next MFF while deferring Ukraine financing and budget decisions to November and December; Zelensky addressed leaders seeking an additional €70bn and further Russia sanctions. The dominant backdrop was a widening transatlantic rift, as Trump announced an "energy ceasefire" with Putin tied to Russian diesel supplies, waived congressionally mandated energy sanctions, and reportedly pressed Kyiv to halt refinery strikes amid Starlink and intelligence-sharing curbs — with US negotiators also proposing to unfreeze some Russian assets, a move European capitals resisted at a Miami meeting. Middle East escalation compounded the energy picture, with von der Leyen citing EUR 106bn in added fossil-fuel import costs, Brent reaching a five-month high, and attacks on Saudi infrastructure and Strait of Hormuz shipping; Belgium separately signaled it will push for EU sanctions over settler attacks on European diplomats in the West Bank. On the Swiss track, Cassis's FDFA published a contested legal opinion on whether EU compensatory measures constitute sanctions, while Bern independently updated its Venezuela asset-freeze list and maintained its ban on Sudanese gold imports.
