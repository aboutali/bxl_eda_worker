This week

Xi, Trump and Putin held their first trilateral summit in Shenzhen, yielding an unspecified US-China pledge on AI regulation but, by Trump's own account, no concrete progress on nuclear arms control or Ukraine, while leaving Europe outside the room and, per SRF analysis, restoring Putin to the great-power circle. Russian pressure on EU territory rose in parallel: cruise missiles landed in Poland, prompting Warsaw to summon the Russian ambassador; drones exploded over EU diplomats in Kyiv; a Russian helicopter breached Finnish airspace after a strike on a sanctioned freighter; and Estonia closed its border over an alleged sabotage plot against a drone maker. The same week, France acknowledged a limited troop deployment in Ukraine and the Council released €1 billion from the European Peace Facility for joint procurement. Brussels also created a dedicated EU Sanctions Office and Sanctions Envoy focused on implementation and anti-circumvention, with direct relevance for Swiss alignment and enforcement of trade flows through Switzerland, while the 20 November EU-UK summit agreed carbon-market linkage, SPS alignment and youth mobility, files that parallel Switzerland's own bilateral package and existing ETS link. The ICJ's first finding of state responsibility for genocide, against Myanmar, held that counterterrorism aims do not exclude genocidal intent, a reading that bears on South Africa v. Israel, and the Qatari Emir's state visit to Bern produced plans for joint peace mediation as Iran escalates drone attacks on Gulf bases and tankers.
