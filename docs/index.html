Today

EU and Western Balkans leaders adopted the Brussels Declaration at their 25 November summit, where von der Leyen and Costa framed enlargement as a strategic imperative, though the EU's push to make Montenegro its first accession in years now faces a resurfacing border dispute with Croatia. Hungarian PM Péter Magyar's meeting with Zelenskyy signals a conditional thaw after the Orbán era that could reduce Budapest's veto leverage over EU Ukraine and Russia sanctions decisions. This matters because the Russia sanctions pipeline continues to move: the EU added 15 military-industrial entities to its Annex IV dual-use export list, the Commission issued guidelines for a Schengen visa ban on individuals who fought in Russia's war, and a leaked draft package would target Russian sports and cultural figures used for propaganda. All three measures are relevant to Swiss alignment, and the visa-ban guidelines apply directly given Switzerland's Schengen association. Separately, the US administration proposed tariffs of up to 15 percent on Switzerland, the EU and China, and the Federal Council is awaiting official notification before responding, while North Korea is closing Switzerland's recently opened Pyongyang liaison office, reportedly over sanctions.
