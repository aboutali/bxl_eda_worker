Today

The European Council's 15-16 October summit produced conclusions spanning Ukraine, the Middle East, defence, enlargement, migration and the next multiannual financial framework, though leaders deferred decisions on Ukraine financing, migration and the long-term budget to November and December. Zelenskyy addressed the summit requesting an additional €70bn as leaders reaffirmed support, a pivot to Europe sharpened by Trump's waiver of congressionally mandated Russia energy sanctions under a diesel-supply deal with Moscow—which Kyiv warned could open wider sanctions relief—and by reported Starlink throttling that has prompted EU lawmakers to press for faster Ukrainian access to bloc satellite capacity. The Council added individuals and entities to its sanctions lists for circumventing existing restrictive measures, a listing Bern will need to assess for alignment, while the EEAS condemned Russian strikes on Ukraine's trade routes as weaponizing food access. Middle East escalation ran parallel: von der Leyen quantified EUR 106bn in added EU fossil-fuel import costs, Brent reached a five-month high of $110 after missile interceptions by Saudi-led forces, and Iranian activity in the Strait of Hormuz increased amid signs of a new US-Iran confrontation. Sixteen member states and the Commission backed a plan permitting border closures and suspended asylum processing during migration surges, an internal file with limited direct Swiss co-signature exposure.
